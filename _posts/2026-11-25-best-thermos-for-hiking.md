## How to choose a hiking thermos

A thermos and an insulated water bottle look alike, but the lid makes the difference for hot drinks. Most heat escapes through the cap, so a true thermos with a tight stopper and a cup-lid usually beats a wide-open water bottle. Outdoor Life saw this clearly: a Hydro Flask Wide Mouth water bottle came in last among their bottles in the hot-water test, even though it's excellent for ice water.

Size matters too. Bigger bottles hold heat longer, but they're heavier in the pack. For a solo day hike, a half-liter to one-liter bottle is plenty. For a group or a long day in the snow, go bigger.

### 1. Stanley Classic Legendary Bottle — best overall

**Why it works:** Outdoor Life named it best overall. Their 1.1-quart bottle still poured water at 121°F after 12 hours, and was about 104°F after 24 hours. The leak-proof screw-in stopper and insulated lid that doubles as a cup make sharing coffee at a trailhead easy, and Outdoor Life expects it to last for years.

**Skip if:** they count ounces. It's a heavy bottle for its size, and in WildSnow's comparison of half-liter bottles, the Stanley was heavier than a Thermos-brand bottle that also held heat a little better.

**Buying tip:** Outdoor Life notes the plastic handle can break if it's smashed on a hard surface, but replacement handles and cups are sold. Sizes run from small bottles up to 2.5 quarts, so pick the size for how many people will be drinking from it.

### 2. Thermos Stainless King — best value, top heat retention

**Why it works:** In Outdoor Life's test, the 40-ounce Stainless King held heat best of all the bottles, still a warm 106°F after 24 hours. It costs less than the other bottles in their test, has a cup-lid, and is dishwasher safe.

**Skip if:** they want the sturdiest hardware. Outdoor Life found the plastic handle and the twist-and-pour stopper less well made than the Stanley's, and the stopper doesn't screw down flush.

**Buying tip:** The 40-ounce size is a good pick for two people on a full winter day. Fill it with hot water for a few minutes before adding coffee, and it starts the hike hotter.

### 3. Zojirushi stainless vacuum bottle — lightest for day hikes

**Why it works:** WildSnow's readers steered its reviewer, a veteran backcountry skier, to Zojirushi's slim bottles. In his comparison, the 500 ml Zojirushi weighed nearly two ounces less than a Thermos-brand bottle of the same size and held heat almost as well over a day. The push-button pour lid lets you sip without unscrewing a cap in the wind.

**Skip if:** they want something for long expeditions. WildSnow's reviewer wasn't sure the mechanical lid was the right choice where a small failure matters, and the bottle doesn't take other stoppers.

**Buying tip:** Zojirushi makes several similar models and sizes, so read the listing for the lid type and capacity. A 12- to 20-ounce size is right for one person on a day hike.
