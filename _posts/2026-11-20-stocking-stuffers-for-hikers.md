## Water

<div class="pick" markdown="1">
### 1. LifeStraw Personal Water Filter

Advnture describes it as roughly the size of a marker, and you drink straight through it from a stream or bottle.

**Why it works:** It's the classic "just in case" filter. It weighs almost nothing, needs no batteries or pumping, and slips into a hip-belt pocket, so a day hiker who runs dry still has a way to drink.

**Skip if:** they backpack and already carry a squeeze or gravity filter. They'll want the water container below instead.

**Buying tip:** Check the listing for exactly what it removes so you can tell them what it's rated for. A note in the stocking that it's a backup, not a replacement for their main filter, helps.

{% include amzn.html asin="B09SBPP9R9" text="Check the LifeStraw on Amazon" %}
</div>

<div class="pick" markdown="1">
### 2. CNOC Vecto Water Container

Treeline Review likes its TPU build, which resists punctures and delamination, and its wide mouth for scooping from streams.

**Why it works:** It folds down to about the size of an empty zip bag and threads onto Sawyer Squeeze and Katadyn BeFree filters, so it becomes a dirty-water bag for the filter they already own.

**Skip if:** they only hike short, well-watered trails with a bottle and never filter.

**Buying tip:** Make sure you pick the thread size that matches their filter; Treeline linked two different Vecto listings.

{% include amzn.html asin="B07QBQ894L" text="Check the CNOC Vecto on Amazon" %}
</div>

<div class="pick" markdown="1">
### 3. Nuun Sport Hydration Tablets

Treeline Review names these its best electrolyte pick for hiking: a slim tube of fizzy tablets in lots of flavors.

**Why it works:** A tube fits in any pocket, and dropping a tablet into a bottle makes plain (or filtered) water easier to keep drinking on long, sweaty climbs.

**Skip if:** they're picky about flavor and you don't know what they like. A gift they won't drink is clutter.

**Buying tip:** Buy a few tubes in different flavors. It's an easy way to fill out a stocking around one bigger item.

{% include amzn.html asin="B019GU4J56" text="Check Nuun Sport on Amazon" %}
</div>

## Light and navigation

<div class="pick" markdown="1">
### 4. Black Diamond Moji R+ Lantern

Treeline Review calls it pocket-sized and points to the rechargeable battery with a stormproof port, plus a hook and magnets for hanging it.

**Why it works:** It turns a dark tent or car-camping table into a usable space, and the color and strobe modes add some fun. Rechargeable means no hunting for batteries before a trip.

**Skip if:** they're an ultralight backpacker who counts every gram; a headlamp already covers them. Treeline also notes the fixed metal handles don't fit every hanging spot.

**Buying tip:** Pair it with a short USB cable that matches their power bank so it's ready to charge on day one.

{% include amzn.html q="Black Diamond Moji R+ lantern" text="Check the Moji R+ on Amazon" %}
</div>

<div class="pick" markdown="1">
### 5. Suunto M-3 Compass

Advnture lists the Suunto M-3 among the top spots in its compass guide.

**Why it works:** Phones die and lose signal. A real baseplate compass is cheap insurance, and it's a gift that nudges a newer hiker toward learning map and compass skills.

**Skip if:** they already carry a compass and know how to use it; one is plenty.

**Buying tip:** Add a printed map of a trail they love to the stocking. A compass with a map is a gift; a compass alone is homework.

## Safety and tools

<div class="pick" markdown="1">
### 6. Adventure Medical Kits Ultralight/Watertight .7

Treeline Review notes it's about 8 ounces in a waterproof silnylon pouch, with wound care, moleskin, duct tape and tweezers.

**Why it works:** Most day hikers carry either nothing or a random baggie. This is an organized, waterproof kit sized for a day or a short overnight, and it's the kind of thing people rarely buy for themselves.

**Skip if:** they lead groups or do long trips and already carry a bigger kit.

**Buying tip:** Look over the contents list with them later and add any personal items they need. A kit only helps if it's in the pack.

Treeline Review includes the Opinel No.8 in its stocking-stuffer gift list.
